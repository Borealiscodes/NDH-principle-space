## ⭐ **2 — Purpose of A13**

A13 exists to ensure that:

- governance actions remain **legible**  
- governance logic remains **non‑recursive**  
- governance outputs remain **non‑expressive**  
- governance decisions remain **non‑hierarchical**  
- governance clarity prevents **authority drift**  

A13 is the invariant that says:

> **If a governance action is unclear, it becomes dangerous.  
> If it becomes dangerous, it becomes authority.  
> Therefore, governance must be clear.**

## ⭐ **3 — Formal Definition**

### ✔ **A13 = Execution Clarity**  
A governance action must be:

- **clear enough** to prevent authority drift  
- **structured enough** to prevent expressive bleed  
- **bounded enough** to prevent altitude confusion  
- **simple enough** to prevent recursion adjacency  
- **explicit enough** to prevent metaphor hijacking  

A13 is the **clarity boundary** that keeps governance from collapsing into hierarchy.

## ⭐ **5 — A13 Operational Rules**

### **5.1 — Clarity Rule**  
Every governance action must be understandable without metaphor.

### **5.2 — Purity Rule**  
Governance language must remain principle‑aligned, not expressive‑aligned.

### **5.3 — Boundary Rule**  
Governance actions must declare their scope explicitly.

### **5.4 — Non‑Recursion Rule**  
Governance actions cannot reference themselves or create loops.

### **5.5 — Non‑Authority Rule**  
Governance actions cannot imply directive inheritance.

A13 prevents:

- expressive geometry → governance contamination  
- metaphor bleed → directive inheritance  
- recursion adjacency → governance loops  
- constellation metaphors → governance altitude confusion  

A13 is the invariant that keeps governance **clear**, **safe**, and **non‑hierarchical**.
