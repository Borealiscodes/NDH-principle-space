# 📘 **Alignment Principle Specification (v1.0)**  
### *Coherence Norms • Ethical Consistency • Decision Harmony • Non‑Hierarchical Integration*

---

## ⭐ **1 — Identity**  
**Name:** Alignment Principle  
**Version:** v1.0  
**Domain:** Principle‑Based Governance Frameworks  
**Role:**  
Ensure coherence of goals, ethical consistency, and decision harmony across all governance artifacts and interactions.

Alignment is the **third principle**, following Clarity and Coordination.

---

## ⭐ **2 — Purpose**  
The Alignment Principle ensures:

- shared goals  
- ethical consistency  
- coherence across decisions  
- stable interpretive direction  
- non‑hierarchical agreement formation  

It prevents:

- drift in intent  
- contradictory decisions  
- implicit authority formation  
- collapse‑style misalignment  
- conceptual fragmentation  

Alignment is the **coherence backbone** of governance.

---

## ⭐ **3 — Formal Definition**  
> **Alignment is the principle that governs coherence of goals, ethical consistency, and decision harmony across governance systems.  
> It ensures that all actions, protocols, and decisions remain directionally consistent without relying on hierarchy or authority.**

Alignment is not an authority.  
Alignment is not a control mechanism.  
Alignment is a **distributed coherence norm**.

---

## ⭐ **4 — Structural Components**

### **4.1 Goal Coherence Layer**  
Defines:

- shared objectives  
- intended outcomes  
- directional consistency  
- scope boundaries  

Goals must be:

- explicit  
- stable  
- non‑hierarchical  
- clarity‑aligned  

---

### **4.2 Ethical Consistency Layer**  
Defines:

- ethical constraints  
- decision boundaries  
- coherence checks  
- alignment review practices  

Ethics must be:

- transparent  
- distributed  
- non‑coercive  
- stable  

---

### **4.3 Decision Harmony Layer**  
Defines:

- how decisions relate  
- how decisions reinforce each other  
- how contradictions are resolved  
- how alignment loops close  

Harmony must be:

- predictable  
- clarity‑compatible  
- coordination‑aware  
- non‑centralized  

---

## ⭐ **5 — Protocols**

### **Protocol A‑1 — Goal Stability**  
Goals must be:

- documented  
- shared  
- stable  
- non‑ambiguous  

Formal invariant:

\[
G_{\text{goal}} = \text{stable}
\]

---

### **Protocol A‑2 — Ethical Coherence**  
Ethical constraints must:

- apply uniformly  
- be visible  
- be non‑hierarchical  
- be consistent across contexts  

---

### **Protocol A‑3 — Decision Harmony**  
Decisions must:

- reinforce shared goals  
- avoid contradiction  
- maintain directional consistency  
- align with clarity and coordination  

---

### **Protocol A‑4 — Mode Purity**  
Alignment must not mix with metaphor.

Formal invariant:

\[
M \not\rightarrow A
\]

Metaphor → Translation → Principle → Governance  
never  
Metaphor → Alignment → Governance.

---

## ⭐ **6 — Invariants**

### **Invariant 1 — Non‑Hierarchy**  
Alignment cannot enforce authority.

### **Invariant 2 — Non‑Centralization**  
No single node may define alignment.

### **Invariant 3 — Non‑Metaphor**  
Alignment cannot contain metaphor constructs.

### **Invariant 4 — Coherence Stability**  
Alignment must remain stable across time.

### **Invariant 5 — Ethical Transparency**  
Ethical constraints must be visible.

---

## ⭐ **7 — Governance Integration**

Alignment integrates into governance through:

- coherence checks  
- ethical consistency reviews  
- decision harmony protocols  
- alignment loops  
- directional stability guidelines  

Formal integration rule:

\[
G = f(C, K, A, R)
\]

Where:

- \(C\) = **Clarity**  
- \(K\) = **Coordination**  
- \(A\) = Alignment  
- \(R\) = **Resilience**  

Alignment is always evaluated **after Coordination** and **before Resilience**.
