# 📘 **Coordination Principle Specification (v1.0)**  
### *Interaction Protocols • Responsibility Mapping • Non‑Hierarchical Flow Logic*

---

## ⭐ **1 — Identity**  
**Name:** Coordination Principle  
**Version:** v1.0  
**Domain:** Principle‑Based Governance Frameworks  
**Role:**  
Define how entities interact, share responsibilities, and maintain non‑hierarchical flow within governance systems.

Coordination is the **second principle**, following Clarity.

---

## ⭐ **2 — Purpose**  
The Coordination Principle ensures:

- stable interaction rules  
- distributed responsibility  
- non‑hierarchical decision pathways  
- predictable communication flow  
- safe interoperability between conceptual systems  

It prevents:

- authority drift  
- routing confusion  
- unilateral decision formation  
- collapse‑style bottlenecks  
- mode contamination  

Coordination is the **interaction backbone** of governance.

---

## ⭐ **3 — Formal Definition**  
> **Coordination is the principle that governs interaction protocols, responsibility distribution, and flow logic across governance systems.  
> It ensures that decisions emerge from structured, non‑hierarchical interaction rather than authority or altitude.**

Coordination is not a routing engine.  
Coordination is not a control layer.  
Coordination is a **distributed protocol set**.

---

## ⭐ **4 — Structural Components**

### **4.1 Interaction Protocol Layer**  
Defines how entities:

- communicate  
- request information  
- provide updates  
- negotiate constraints  
- synchronize decisions  

Interaction must be:

- predictable  
- non‑coercive  
- clarity‑aligned  
- responsibility‑aware  

---

### **4.2 Responsibility Mapping Layer**  
Defines:

- who holds which responsibilities  
- how responsibilities are shared  
- how responsibilities shift  
- how responsibilities are documented  

Responsibilities must be:

- explicit  
- distributed  
- non‑hierarchical  
- stable  

---

### **4.3 Flow Logic Layer**  
Defines:

- how decisions move through the system  
- how information propagates  
- how coordination loops close  
- how conflicts are resolved  

Flow logic must be:

- transparent  
- stable  
- non‑centralized  
- clarity‑compatible  

---

## ⭐ **5 — Protocols**

### **Protocol K‑1 — Distributed Responsibility**  
Responsibilities must be:

- shared  
- documented  
- non‑centralized  
- non‑authoritative  

Formal invariant:

\[
R \neq \text{central node}
\]

---

### **Protocol K‑2 — Interaction Predictability**  
All interactions must follow:

- defined steps  
- defined pacing  
- defined constraints  

No ad‑hoc or ambiguous interaction patterns.

---

### **Protocol K‑3 — Flow Stability**  
Decision flows must:

- avoid bottlenecks  
- avoid altitude metaphors  
- avoid central routing  
- maintain clarity alignment  

Flow stability prevents collapse.

---

### **Protocol K‑4 — Mode Purity**  
Coordination must not mix with metaphor.

Formal invariant:

\[
M \not\rightarrow K
\]

Metaphor → Translation → Principle → Governance  
never  
Metaphor → Coordination → Governance.

---

## ⭐ **6 — Invariants**

### **Invariant 1 — Non‑Hierarchy**  
Coordination cannot create authority.

### **Invariant 2 — Non‑Centralization**  
No single node may control flow.

### **Invariant 3 — Non‑Metaphor**  
Coordination cannot contain metaphor constructs.

### **Invariant 4 — Flow Transparency**  
All flow logic must be visible.

### **Invariant 5 — Responsibility Stability**  
Responsibilities cannot drift without documentation.

---

## ⭐ **7 — Governance Integration**

Coordination integrates into governance through:

- interaction protocols  
- responsibility maps  
- decision flow diagrams  
- conflict resolution guidelines  
- synchronization rules  

Formal integration rule:

\[
G = f(C, K, A, R)
\]

Where:

- \(C\) = **Clarity**  
- \(K\) = Coordination  
- \(A\) = **Alignment**  
- \(R\) = **Resilience**  

Coordination is always evaluated **after Clarity** and **before Alignment**.
